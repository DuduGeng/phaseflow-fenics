Profile

I am a PhD researcher in Materials Engineering at South East Technological University in Carlow, with a strong interest in sustainable materials, circular design and the transformation of waste materials into higher-value applications. My background is primarily scientific rather than traditional jewellery-making, but I am interested in exploring how materials science can connect with craft, public engagement and creative sustainability.

Materials background

My research training helps me understand how materials behave, transform and acquire value through processing, design and context. This gives me a scientific foundation for exploring recycled, upcycled and unconventional materials.

Creative direction

I would like to develop an emerging creative practice that uses material storytelling to make sustainability visible, personal and wearable.

My aim is not to present myself as an established jewellery designer, but as an emerging creative and materials researcher who wants to learn, experiment and contribute to sustainable making.

Creative Direction

The central idea of this portfolio is to reimagine discarded or low-value materials as small wearable objects that can communicate environmental responsibility, circular thinking and local material stories.

Concept Ideas

These are early-stage concepts for sustainable wearable pieces. They are intentionally simple and exploratory, designed for hands-on experimentation during a short creative residency.

Concept 01
Layered Fragment Brooch
Recycled fragments

Layered Fragment Brooch

A brooch made from small pieces of discarded plastic, packaging or thin metal, layered to show how waste can be reorganised into a new visual structure. The piece would communicate transformation: from scattered fragments to a coherent wearable object.

Concept 02
Bound Fibre Pendant
Soft structure

Bound Fibre Pendant

A pendant created by binding textile offcuts, fibres or cord-like waste into a compact form. This idea explores hand-making, repair and visible connection as metaphors for circular design.

Concept 03
Waste Map Necklace
Material storytelling

Waste Map Necklace

A necklace or small wearable series where each material sample represents a different waste stream. The design would act as a small material map, helping viewers understand where materials come from and how they might be reimagined.

Residency Learning Plan

During the CUBE Craft Academy, I would like to move from scientific understanding of materials toward practical making, creative communication and collaborative learning.

1

Explore

Observe available recycled and unconventional materials; test simple joining, layering and surface approaches.

2

Develop

Refine a wearable concept through mentoring, peer feedback and hands-on experimentation.

3

Present

Complete small wearable pieces and explain the material story, sustainability idea and learning process.

What I hope to gain

  • Practical experience in sustainable jewellery-making.
  • Confidence in presenting creative work.
  • A stronger connection between materials science and public-facing sustainability practice.
  • International creative network and peer feedback.

What I can contribute

  • A materials engineering perspective.
  • Interest in circular design and waste-to-value transformation.
  • Experience communicating complex ideas in international settings.
  • Curiosity, openness and active participation.
